\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

% Summarize the central findings, state the limitations of the study, and
% outline concrete future work/extensions.

We implemented feed-forward neural networks from scratch, verified the gradients from
backpropagation against automatic differentiation, and compared the networks with linear and
logistic regression on two problems.

On Runge's function, small networks approximated the true function about an order of
magnitude better than the polynomial models of Project~1, which oscillate near the ends of the
interval. Small networks were best, since larger ones fit the noise; for ReLU networks, an
$\ell_1$ or $\ell_2$ penalty removed most of this overfitting, while sigmoid networks did not
benefit from it. On MNIST, a network with four hidden ReLU layers reached a test accuracy of
97.7\%, against 92.6\% for logistic regression, and corrected most of the confusion between
digits that share strokes. Here width mattered more than depth, and a decaying learning rate
and early stopping gave a clear improvement. Our implementation performed on par with or
better than Scikit-Learn for both models.

The networks were the most accurate models for both tasks, but also the most expensive and
the most sensitive to their hyperparameters. Their flexibility paid off most for images, where
good features are hard to design by hand. As discussed in \cref{subsec:critical-evaluation},
the conclusions rest on single training runs, a small regression test set and coarse search
grids.

Natural extensions are to repeat the experiments over several random initializations to
measure the variation between runs, to search wider networks for MNIST, and to use the
learning rate schedule also for regression. Dropout and batch normalization could reduce
overfitting and ease the training of deep sigmoid networks, and convolutional networks would
exploit the spatial structure of the images that a fully connected network ignores. Finally,
the methods could be tested on harder data, such as Runge's function in two or three
dimensions and the Fashion-MNIST data set.
